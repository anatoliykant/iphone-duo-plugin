# iPhone Duo design rules — D1–D29 with sources

Sources: Apple HIG "Designing for iPhone Duo" (page published 2026-09-09, change log unchanged as of 2026-09-16; JSON: `https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo.json`, sections Anatomy · Device poses · Best practices · Dynamic layouts (Reserved regions, Split views, Arrangement views) · Vertical controls); tech talks **111466** "Design for iPhone Duo" (Apple Design team), **111462** "Raise the bar with iPhone Duo" (design chapters), **111463** "Strike a pose with adaptive layouts on iPhone Duo" (design chapters), **111461** "Prepare your app for iPhone Duo". Timecodes point into the official transcript on `https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/<id>/` — a chapter start where the source is the chapter, the spoken sentence where the source is a quote. Talk quotes were re-verified against those transcripts on 2026-09-16.

Added 2026-09-21: the developer article "[Preparing your app for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo)" (cited as **article**), and the two iPhone Duo **Group Labs** — Meet with Apple **285** (2026-09-16) and **286** (2026-09-17), cited as `lab 285 MM:SS`. The labs are Apple engineers and designers answering questions live, transcribed from **auto-generated captions**: good for intent and for what Apple actually recommends, never for an API spelling, and a panelist's "I believe" is not a rule.

**Citation discipline.** The HIG page has **no Do/Don't blocks** — every guideline is a bold lead-in sentence inside a paragraph. Cite it as `HIG <section> "<bold lead-in>"` and quote only text that is literally on the page; never label a citation "HIG Do". Anything in quotation marks below is verbatim; a paraphrase is written without quotes. Rule ids are stable — new rules append, they never renumber.

Facts a reviewer needs (all from those sources): outer display 5.4" compact width (like other iPhones, but wider and shorter); inner display 7.6" **regular × regular**; system bars (navigation, toolbar, tab bar, status bar, Dynamic Island) move to a **vertical edge** on the outer display and on the inner display in landscape; inner display in **portrait keeps horizontal bars**; in Split View "each one places controls along its outer edge, so the left app has controls on the left"; the bars follow the hardware, so they "stay on the same side in right-to-left languages"; when partially folded the hinge divides the inner display into two usable regions; the outer front camera "is in the corner and is always visible, vertically aligned with controls on the side" and "expands into the Dynamic Island for Live Activities", the inner camera is under the display and "stays hidden until the camera is active"; the system already moves alerts, context menus and sheets around the fold; every app takes part in Split View. Points in this file are **derived** — Apple publishes pixels only: outer 466 × 678; inner **626 × 890 in portrait** (horizontal bars; folded = table pose, hinge horizontal at y ≈ 445) and **890 × 626 in landscape** (vertical bars; folded = book pose, hinge **vertical at x ≈ 445**); Split View half ≈ 313 × 890 portrait or ≈ 445 × 626 landscape. The "fold band" is therefore a vertical strip in landscape frames and a horizontal strip in portrait frames.

Severity legend: B = BLOCKER, M = MUST-FIX, S = SHOULD, N = NOTE.

## Coverage of the design set

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Both displays are designed: at least one compact (outer) and one regular (inner) frame per screen that ships on Duo | only phone-width frames exist, or inner-display frames are missing for a screen | B | HIG Best practices "Build your app to resize" / "Create a consistent experience across displays"; 111466 3:42 |
| D2 | Frames use realistic Duo geometry, labelled derived, and are classified by `absoluteBoundingBox`, not by name | frames at arbitrary sizes; a "Duo" frame identical to an iPad frame; frames named "Portrait" that are 890 × 626, or "Split View" frames that show the app's own folded two-pane arrangement rather than two apps | N | Apple publishes pixels 1398 × 2034 / 1878 × 2670 only (specs page); points derived |
| D3 | No layout designed per **pose** (book, table, tent) — only per size class | separate "folded"/"table" screens with different content or controls | B | HIG Device poses: "Supporting the device's various poses doesn't mean designing a custom layout for each one: instead, use Size classes so your app adapts naturally as it changes size" and "Don't reinvent your app when it resizes"; 111466 0:28 |
| D4 | Split View half (~313 pt) considered for screens with dense layouts | a regular-width screen that cannot collapse to a compact half without losing functions | S | HIG Best practices "Build your app to resize": "Because the device has two displays and supports a wide range of poses and Split View multitasking, your app can appear at many different sizes"; 111461 6:06 |

## Consistency and hierarchy

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D5 | Same functionality and element states on both displays; the inner display shows **additional hierarchy** (Mail: list *or* message closed, both open) | features exist on one display only; inner display just enlarges the outer layout | M | HIG Best practices "Create a consistent experience across displays": "Keep functionality and the state of elements the same between displays. Maintain your app's information hierarchy, but show an additional level of hierarchy on the larger inner display if it makes sense for your content"; 111466 7:34 |
| D6 | Inner display uses width for structure — split views, two columns, sidebars, inspectors — not for stretched rows or longer text lines | forms/text stretched edge to edge on 626 pt; single column where a list + detail fits | S | HIG Split views: "a split view expands on the inner display and collapses to a single pane on the outer display"; 111466 7:44 "Split views are a great option. They let you surface multiple levels of your app hierarchy at the same time." |
| D7 | Functionality does not depend on pose or orientation; controls may overflow, content may move, access stays the same | an action only reachable in one pose | M | HIG Best practices "Maintain the same functionality across device poses": "Provide access to the same controls and content regardless of how someone holds or views the device" |

## Vertical controls (bars)

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D8 | Standard system bars are used and drawn where the system puts them: **vertical** on the outer display (and inner landscape), horizontal on inner portrait; the mockup does not hand-draw a bottom tab bar / top nav bar on outer-display frames | custom bar chrome, bars at the wrong edge for the class, "we'll keep our own bar" | B | HIG Vertical controls, best practice "Follow the system's vertical layout for toolbars, tab bars, and navigation controls" and "In general, don't override the default bar placement": "The position of controls on the vertical axis is one of the core patterns of iPhone Duo … reinforces the unified platform experience"; 111462 0:28, 2:00 |
| D9 | Bar item order on the vertical bar: primary navigation (Back / Close) at the top, then prominent actions (Done), then remaining groups in their original groupings; overflow items are the least important | Done above Back; primary actions designed into overflow; hand-drawn gaps between the top and bottom groups | M | HIG Vertical controls "Follow the standard placement order for toolbar items": "Reserve the top of the vertical axis for primary navigation controls, like Back or Close, followed by prominent actions, like Done" and "the system provides a vertical space between items from the top and bottom bars to keep them distinct"; 111462 4:29 |
| D10 | Every bar item has a **symbol and a title**; symbol-only rendering preferred; badges instead of inline text ("Inbox 7"). **Tab items need an icon** — a title-only tab does go vertical, but a vertical tab bar draws icons and reveals labels only while a finger scrubs across it | text-only buttons in bars (they stay horizontal and eat space); labels as the only affordance; a tab bar designed with words and no icons | M | HIG Vertical controls "Provide both a title and a symbol for each toolbar item that isn't text-only" (the system "uses the title in overflow menus and expanded forms") and "Keep text-based buttons to a minimum": "Labels that include text stay in a horizontal bar, so prefer a symbol wherever one works"; 111462 5:56, 9:00 |
| D11 | Related items grouped; no manual fixed spacing between bar items; own "more" actions live in the system overflow, and other menus get their own symbol | custom ellipsis menus, hand-spaced items, a second "more" menu next to the system one | M | HIG Vertical controls "Group related toolbar items instead of spacing them manually" ("avoid adding fixed spacing yourself") and "Use the system overflow menu": "Reserve the ellipsis symbol for overflow, and give other menus a distinct symbol"; 111462 11:40 |
| D12 | Frequently used actions (Compose, New) and status-conveying items (badges) are shown as staying visible when the bar compresses (landscape outer, keyboard) | mockup shows them buried, or no compressed state considered for a 6+ item bar | S | HIG Vertical controls "Prioritize frequently used toolbar items to keep them easily available": "Items overflow from bottom to top by default" and "Preserve frequently used actions first, like Compose in Mail or New Note in Notes, and keep controls that convey important status, like items with badges, visible longer"; 111462 13:10 |
| D13 | Opt-out of vertical bars only for single-page immersive screens (Calculator-like) or a sheet with a single Close button | opt-out drawn for ordinary content screens | B | HIG Vertical controls "Consider using the full display width for interfaces where bars aren't necessary": "works well for visual, immersive interfaces that don't scroll, as long as nothing conflicts with the Dynamic Island or the status bar"; 111462 14:21 |
| D19 | Controls that belong to a content area other than the trailing one stay **with that area**, not on the side bar (Mail: list controls sit above the leading pane) | on a two-pane inner frame every control is collected onto the vertical bar, so list actions look like detail actions | S | HIG Vertical controls "Locate controls near the content they affect": "When controls belong to a content area other than the one along the trailing edge, keep them with that area rather than moving them to the side" |
| D20 | Split View frames put each app's controls on its **outer** edge, and content is laid out asymmetrically — the opposite edge may carry the neighbour's controls | Split View frames drawn with both bars on the inner edges, or symmetric left/right margins copied from a phone frame | M | HIG Vertical controls: "each one places controls along its outer edge, so the left app has controls on the left" and "Account for asymmetry in your layouts": "Use safe areas to make sure controls don't cover your content, including controls on the opposite edge" |
| D21 | In right-to-left languages the bar stays on the **same hardware side** — it does not mirror | an Arabic/Hebrew mockup flips the vertical bar to the other edge | M | HIG Vertical controls: "Because controls on the vertical axis stay aligned with the hardware, they hold the same position relative to the camera on the outer display, and stay on the same side in right-to-left languages" |
| D22 | For a screen with both a toolbar and a tab bar, the mockup says which one survives compression: navigation-focused → toolbar items go to overflow (system default); task-oriented → tab bar minimizes | a crowded bar with no stated compression preference on a screen that has both | S | HIG Vertical controls "When space is limited, preserve either the toolbar or tab bar based on the experience that the view provides": navigation-focused = "the default bar compression behavior", task-oriented = "minimize the tab bar to preserve the toolbar actions" |
| D23 | Mixing full-width and inset is allowed and is **not** a D8/D13 finding: a background image or header may span the display while scrollable content stays inset — the implementation has an API for exactly this (`backgroundExtensionEffect()` / `UIBackgroundExtensionView`) | a reviewer flags a full-bleed hero as a bar violation, or the designer insets artwork that could span | N | HIG Vertical controls: "You can also combine both approaches, letting a background image or header span the full width while scrollable content stays inset"; 111466 7:20 "You can also mix both approaches, a full-width background image or header with scrollable foreground content that's inset" |

## Fold avoidance and reserved regions

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D14 | On inner-display frames no interactive or semantic element sits on the fold band (vertical strip at x ≈ 445 in landscape, horizontal at y ≈ 445 in portrait): buttons, text, faces, QR codes, input fields, drag handles, small icons. Backgrounds and scrolling content may cross. **Severity depends on the folded state:** B when the folded frames are missing or still straddle; S when the folded frames clear the band and only the flat state hugs the line (the crease is physical in every pose) | a primary button, form field or title centred on the fold in the folded frames (B); CTA chevron / links / timeline spine on the centre line in flat frames only (S) | B / S | HIG Reserved regions: "When the device is partially open, the folding region divides the inner display into multiple usable regions" and "Use the `ReservedRegion` API to keep important elements clear of the center if the system doesn't move them automatically"; 111466 9:51 "keep interactive elements out of the curve region as much as possible"; 111463 1:29 chapter "Designing around the hinge" — a photo spread across the fold, at 1:54, "no longer reads as one continuous image" (talk, not the HIG page) |
| D15 | Grids use an **even** number of columns on the inner display so a fold divides them cleanly | 3- or 5-column grids on regular frames | M | HIG Reserved regions "Adapt your layout when the device folds": "In a grid-style layout, prefer an even number of columns so content divides cleanly" |
| D16 | Displacement is small and local: alerts/sheets move to the trailing side in book pose, content goes top / controls bottom in table pose; the screen is not redesigned around the fold. Bar positions stay comparable between poses | drastic rearrangement between flat and folded frames; controls disappearing; a control that lives at the top of the vertical bar reappearing at the bottom of a horizontal bar | S | HIG Reserved regions "Avoid extreme layout changes as people fold the device": "Move only what's necessary … favor small adjustments over rearrangement"; HIG Vertical controls "Keep controls consistent across device poses": "keep controls' relative positions as similar as possible"; 111463 2:26, 4:00 |
| D17 | Content respects the outer camera (corner, always visible, **vertically aligned with the side controls**, and it "expands into the Dynamic Island for Live Activities") and the inner camera when active — nothing important under them; foreground inside the safe area, only backgrounds extend past it | title, avatar or button placed in the camera corner; content designed edge-to-edge on the camera side; a bar item drawn where the camera sits | B | HIG Anatomy + Reserved regions (three regions: outer camera, inner camera, folding region); "The inner camera … stays hidden until the camera is active"; 111461 8:55 "Align your interactive or visible foreground content to the safe area, while background content may extend past the safe area." |
| D18 | Sheets: on the **outer** display they carry vertical controls by default (a single-Close sheet may opt out); on the **inner** display the axis follows the sheet's placement — horizontal for centered or leading, vertical for trailing — so the mockup says which placement it wants; when folded they slide aside from the fold, which the system does for you | sheets designed with a bottom-only action row on outer frames; sheet content centred on the fold; an inner-display sheet whose bars contradict its own placement | S | Article: "On the outer display, the system presents bars vertically for sheets by default … For sheets on the inner display, the system presents the toolbar horizontally for centered or leading placements, and vertically for trailing placements"; HIG Reserved regions: "Components like alerts, context menus, and sheets automatically move to account for the fold"; 111466 8:36 "Sheet behavior". A sheet that is mostly controls may reasonably keep horizontal bars — lab 286 46:47 calls a vertical bar there "Distracting almost" |
| D24 | Standard components are **not** hand-adapted to the fold: alerts, context menus and sheets move themselves, and split views adjust their own column widths and margins | the file carries bespoke "folded" variants of a system alert / menu / sheet, or hand-tuned split-view column widths per pose | S | HIG Reserved regions: "Many system components automatically adapt to reserved regions … while larger components like Split views adapt their columns' width and margins to match the symmetry of the inner display" |

## Arrangement views

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D25 | A two-view screen states which arrangement it is, and the folded frames match it: **split** (side by side or stacked — "splits horizontally when the arrangement is wider than it is tall, and splits vertically when the arrangement is taller than it is wide") or **overlay** (one over the other when flat; when partially folded the article is specific — "the overlay arrangement places the primary view in the trailing or bottom part of the display relative to the fold, and the secondary view in the leading or top part") | a flat frame showing two panes with folded frames that contradict both behaviours; an overlaid panel drawn as still overlaying in the folded frame; a side-by-side pair whose folded frame keeps the same proportions instead of following the fold | S | HIG Arrangement views: definitions of the split and overlay arrangements; "Consider an arrangement view when your layout already resembles one" — side-by-side / stacked (HStack, VStack) → split; layered (ZStack) → overlay |
| D26 | Navigation chrome (tab bar, navigation/split-view bars) sits **around** the arrangement, never inside one of its two views | a mockup that gives each of the two panes its own tab bar or nav bar, or puts the arrangement above the app's navigation | M | HIG Arrangement views "Keep navigation outside of arrangement views": "place navigation containers like navigation split views and tab views around it rather than within it" |

## Poses over time

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D28 | The frames for two poses are recognisably the same screen, and the move between them is small enough to follow: same elements, same reading order, only position changes | a "seated" frame that is a different screen from the flat one; elements that appear or vanish between poses with nothing to connect them | S | Lab 285 42:18: "if you have one pose UI in seated pose and it's entirely different than when you're flat, you have to think about how that actually transitions. Can be disorienting"; 56:57, on the biggest design mistake seen so far — "trying to design for every pose and pulling back from that"; 42:07 "don't feel stressed about having to sizzle up every single pose" |
| D29 | A tab bar inside a sheet stays inside the sheet when the tabs choose what the sheet shows; it separates only when each tab owns a different sheet | a Find My-style sheet whose tabs are pulled out to the side bar on the inner display although every tab just repopulates the same sheet | S | Lab 285 52:30: "if the tabs apply to all of the states of the sheet, then it makes sense for them to be together … Any tab you go to is populating the sheet with the contents of that tab"; 52:49, "there might be a different app where like a tab has its own sheet. And maybe they do make sense to be separated" |

## Games

| Id | Rule | Fail if | Sev | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D27 | A game may lock to portrait or landscape, but every pose fills the screen; when resizing, text and control sizes stay as consistent as possible; changing the aspect ratio beats letterboxing or pillarboxing, and unavoidable padding carries artwork | letterboxed frames for the inner display; HUD text that shrinks with the viewport; empty black padding bars | M | HIG Best practices "Make your game playable in every device pose": "be sure to fill the screen as the device pose changes. When resizing, keep text and control sizes as consistent as possible. Prefer changing the aspect ratio over letterboxing or pillarboxing … add artwork to the padding area to help the experience feel full screen" |

## What Apple does **not** say (do not invent)

- No Duo-specific typography sizes, tap-target sizes, spacing or margins — apply the general HIG values (`hig-general-checklist.md`).
- No hinge width, angle thresholds or safe-area numbers.
- No "compatibility mode" in any Apple wording. *Letterboxing* does describe an unrebuilt app, but only in the labs, never in the HIG — lab 285 23:32, "if you're linking against Xcode 26 on the Inner display … you'll be Letterboxed", narrowing with Xcode 27 and gone with 27.1. Cite the lab for it, not the HIG.
- No Do/Don't lists on the Duo page, and no sentence about content "spanning the fold" — the page's wording is the folding region "divides the inner display into multiple usable regions". The spine metaphor is 111463 1:29, a talk.
